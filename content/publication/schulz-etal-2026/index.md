abstract: 'The Greenland Ice Sheet is melting at an accelerating pace under climate
  change, adding large additional amounts of freshwater to the ocean. This additional
  freshwater input has consequences not only for global sea-level rise but potentially
  also for the structure of the North Atlantic water column and, by extension, for
  the global overturning circulation. While recent model studies projecting a potential
  weakening or shutdown of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) have
  received widespread attention, the underlying models carry inherent uncertainties,
  particularly with respect to how freshwater from Greenland is represented and released
  in these model frameworks. In this paper, we provide an accessible review of the
  current state of modeling approaches, ranging from ice-sheet to fjord, shelf, and
  global scales. We discuss their respective strengths and limitations, illustrate
  key sources of uncertainty, and outline what this may mean for the interpretation
  of the simulated projections, to provide interdisciplinary researchers with a summary
  of our current understanding and highlight knowledge gaps on the effect of Greenland
  ice sheet melt on the ocean.'
